Figure 1 A static right arm anatomical reference

Ten named BodyParts3D 4.0 surfaces in their original atlas pose, shown as
bones alone and with selected muscle surfaces. Rendering colors are added
for explanation. The official release is strongly polygon-reduced, and its
geometry is a reference atlas rather than a subject-specific elbow model.
No validated joint axis, muscle mechanics or tissue deformation is supplied.
BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science licensed under CC
Attribution 4.0 International. Changes: subset, unit conversion and rendering.

Alternative text: Two anterior-facing views of a straight right-arm atlas.
The left shows humerus, radius and ulna. The right adds brachialis,
brachioradialis, both biceps heads and three triceps heads with keyed colors.

Figure 2 A recorded isometric elbow trial

Original plot of OpenArm Multisensor 2.0 participant 2, trial 1b, at a fixed
90-degree elbow posture with supinated forearm. Curves show brachioradialis
ultrasound-derived thickness, biceps sEMG and wrist-contact force. They are
preprocessed trial-calibrated signals expressed in dimensionless units;
zero refers to relaxed calibration and one to the calibration contraction.
Half-second bins contain sample-weighted means. The sensors measure
different quantities and, for ultrasound and sEMG, different muscles.
A single example does not define a general force law or calibrate the atlas.
Hallock et al. (2021), DOI 10.1109/TNSRE.2021.3133813, data CC BY 4.0.
Changes: selected trial and retained segment, relative time, display binning.

Alternative text: Three aligned time-series plots over about 86 seconds.
Normalized thickness, sEMG and wrist-contact force all vary within the
same held elbow posture, but their amplitudes and profiles differ. Some
thickness and sEMG values exceed the calibration value of one. No curve
is labeled as an individual-muscle force or an inferred activation state.
