A compatible load path

Energy determines the slips

U = ½K1u² + ½K2(δ−v)²
    + ½CLv² + ½CR(δ−u)²

u* = CRδ/(K1+CR)
v* = K2δ/(K2+CL)

Keff = K1CR/(K1+CR)
     + K2CL/(K2+CL)
Tleft = Tright = Keff δ

Material property → transmitted force

K = EA/L and C = GAs/h, with E/G in Pa, areas in m² and lengths in m. Force is N; recoverable energy is J. E is the reduced axial modulus of a guided element.

Both interfaces zero disconnect the force path. Increasing a finite interface stiffness increases Keff. A perfect tie is only an infinite-stiffness limit.

Geometry: A1=A2=As,L=As,R=1 mm², L1=L2=10 mm, hL=hR=0.2 mm. These are authored macro-elements, not real single-fibre dimensions. Only axial endpoint DOFs remain; transverse motion is guided, normal/moment and rotation mechanics are omitted.

Measured motivation, distinct scope

Street (1983) recorded lateral tension transmission in splinted single frog semitendinosus myofibres. Only the original abstract was verified. It motivates a load path; no modulus, geometry or human/bundle/whole-muscle calibration is extracted. Zhang and Gao (2012) supplies computational motivation, rather than measured parameters. Bower §§3.2.6–3.2.7 supplies small-strain elastic conventions.

This bilateral passive linear network has two localized macro-links, not a distributed tissue field. It adds interface compatibility after architecture-to-force. Finite-strain serial mechanics, SLS history, active forces, physiology, fluid/perfusion and the full anatomical capstone remain separate.
