# Mechanical closure before another loaded arm solve

**Recommend an additive active-stress/Hill family with an explicit reference-fascicle/optimal-length map, complete passive load paths and a verified full-displacement mixed pressure formulation. First test reference normalization and incompressible admissibility locally; then perform an isolated full P2/P1 prism-to-taper equilibrium experiment.** Do not choose a stabilizing mechanism or numerical coefficient merely because it produces a converged arm. The prototype below is an ablation of reference/pressure conventions, not a replacement production law or a physiological parameter estimate.

## Original sources and parameter meaning

[Holzbaur, Murray and Delp (2005), methods/Table 1](https://nmbl.stanford.edu/publications/pdf/Holzbaur2005.pdf) derive model maximum forces from PCSA and specific tension, selecting elbow/shoulder **140 N/cm²=1.4 MPa** to match joint moments. Keep that model-derived provenance. Local Arm26's BICshort additionally declares optimal fiber length **0.1321 m**, tendon slack length **0.1923 m** and maximum contraction velocity **10 optimal fiber lengths/s**. Those reference-model inputs are distinct from measured patient architecture. Kenoma's fitted **8.708387 MPa** peak first-Piola coefficient, operating Cauchy stress **a sigma0 fL lambda/J**, and a geometric V/L must not be equated with the source specific tension or PCSA.

[Blemker, Pinsky and Delp (2005), equations 1–9/Table 2](https://nmbl.stanford.edu/publications/pdf/Blemker2005.pdf) use deviatoric fiber/shear measures, an explicit optimum stretch **1.4 relative to their reference**, and separate passive aponeurosis/fascia properties. Their muscle shear constants are 500 Pa and bulk modulus 10 MPa; these are model inputs in a particular long-head architecture, not replacement values for this atlas. The model's architectural ablations and quasi-static scope justify isolating reference and support effects; they do not establish a dynamic viscosity or universal stabilization coefficient. The declared prototype benchmark 1.4 tests normalization sensitivity only.

[Ambrosi and Pezzuto's original author preprint (2011), sections 2–4](https://www.mate.polimi.it/biblioteca/add/qmox/21-2011.pdf), published as [Journal of Elasticity 107 (2012)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10659-011-9351-4), compares additive active stress with **F=Fe Fa**. Frame indifference and ellipticity must be assessed for the total law. Holding an invertible Fa fixed transfers passive properties through the elastic map, but does not supply a skeletal force-length/velocity calibration or a law for Fa's evolution. Their physiological examples are cardiac. Replacing a descending skeletal force-length curve by a cardiac monotone law would discard the phenomenon being modeled.

[Klotz, Bleiler and Röhrle's physiology-guided active-stress/strain classification (2021)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2021.685531/full) distinguishes parallel and serial rheological interpretations and combined models. Thus active strain is not a parameter-free cure interchangeable with active stress. It changes activation-dependent passive/shear behavior and needs an independently justified contraction/distortion law, including a determinant constraint if contraction is volume-preserving. Match uniaxial force, transverse/shear response and rate behavior before selecting it. Do not identify an active distortion amplitude with neural activation by fiat.

[Saini and Röhrle (2022), original musculotendon constitutive implementation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169260722005338) provides a nearly incompressible active-stress framework with force-length and force-velocity behavior, parameter characterization and activation input. Its paper distinguishes stiffness, in-fiber passive behavior and joint movement. This is evidence for a component-based closure/validation workflow, not permission to import another muscle's coefficients. The previously retained [measured-fiber property map](../measured-fibre-property-map.md) documents preparation/temperature/type dependencies and missing fields for a human-biceps rate calibration.

[Maas, Ellis, Ateshian and Weiss (2012), FEBio section 3.2](https://febio.org/site/uploads/maas_jbme_2012.pdf) describe mixed/three-field treatment of near-incompressible elements and distinguish element technologies. A mixed formulation addresses volume constraints/discretization; it cannot cure constitutive ellipticity loss. Full P2 displacement with continuous P1 pressure is a candidate, not a stability certificate for this mesh. The earlier pressure-count bound is insufficient: verify coupling rank, scaled inf-sup behavior, boundary/pressure conventions and mesh refinement.

## Recommended model and controlled solver experiment

Keep an additive active contractile contribution for the first full-space comparison because its source force/length/velocity meanings are explicit and it preserves the inherited modeling family. Treat its potential at fixed activation as a solve device, not passive stored energy. Retain real physical tangents, including negative slopes. For the first quasi-static diagnostic use fV=1 and zero material-rate terms explicitly; this is not a physically validated dynamic release model. Before returning to dynamics, map contraction velocity in fiber lengths/s, contractile state and series compliance; calibrate force-velocity/rate effects against appropriate preparations. Material viscosity or an internal length scale is required only if a supported rate/nonlocal closure is selected, and then its units, dissipation and mesh/time independence must be demonstrated. Joint damping cannot substitute for missing local muscle physics.

Use the same prism and tapered geometry, quadrature, ideal caps and fixed stress coefficients in an **isolated full P2/P1 finite-K mixed experiment**, with the original restricted results as controls. Declare the mixed functional **Pi=integral [Wiso/passive/active + p log J − p²/(2K)] dV0 − external work** for the first finite-K comparator. Here p has Pa units and the weak constitutive relation is **log J=p/K**. Pressure sign and Cauchy conversion are explicit: this p contributes **p/J I**, so it is not silently the conventional negative-pressure multiplier. This functional preserves the existing logarithmic penalty in the continuous pressure-elimination limit; a finite P1 projection changes the discretization and must be labeled. Exact incompressibility is a distinct limit with its own boundary compatibility/gauge handling. Do not increase K to force a desired picture.

Only after force/volume-space qualification should the source-backed fiber reference map and passive architectural supports enter, one intervention at a time. Require the original passive aponeurosis/tendon/fascia load paths and their rest lengths/areas to be represented consistently; sparse authored strips and frozen targets are not validated tissue supports. Releasing ideal caps, adding sheet compliance and changing fiber normalization simultaneously would prevent causal attribution. Compare active stress/strain only after matching their declared uniaxial reference behavior; then use shear/transverse and shortening-rate tests to distinguish them.

Predeclare acceptance for that next solver experiment:

- Independent **full free nodal force ≤1e-4 N**, projection/assembly agreement ≤2e-6 N, and direct cap reaction versus admissible whole-body virtual work within a residual-derived bound ≤1e-3 N. No generalized-force target fit before this passes.
- Mixed constitutive residual: mass-matrix-normalized **RMS(log J−p/K) ≤1e-6**, independent quadrature replay, complete pressure-space rank report and scaled inf-sup estimate across at least two spatial refinements. A nonzero reference inf-sup estimate alone is insufficient.
- Positive original corner/integration determinants and complete existing finite geometry gates. For a candidate near-incompressible tissue state require sampled **0.98≤J≤1.02** and report unsampled/curved-element limitations. This 2% band is a declared engineering diagnostic criterion, not a biological measurement.
- At matched accepted states require cap force difference ≤1%, maximum physical nodal difference ≤0.5 mm and sampled-J extrema difference ≤0.005 across a predeclared refinement pair. Failures remain failures, with unchanged budgets and rollback.
- Classify curvature only at a stationary accepted state, using actual constraints and true physical tangent. Preserve negative directions; report whether they survive incompressibility constraints. Do not replace the tangent by a positive algorithmic matrix to claim stability.

Those are prospective, measurable gates, not results already achieved. Full arm dynamics additionally needs matched timestep, contact history and energy/work checks. Whole-envelope credibility and skin remain blocked.

## Smallest implemented step

The [local protocol](protocol.md) first compares reference normalization and incompressibility-admissible rank-one curvature at saved fields, with exact stress/tangent derivations. It also checks pressure equivalence of full-stretch versus isochoric active potentials at J=1. Those two potentials coincide on the incompressible manifold; a purely hydrostatic stress difference can be absorbed by the pressure multiplier. Changing active volumetric convention alone therefore cannot automatically remove a constrained descending-limb problem. At finite K and J≠1 the models differ, so it must not be called a harmless numerical projection. No production material or new equilibrium is introduced by this diagnostic.
