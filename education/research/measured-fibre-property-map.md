For every future numeric input, retain structural scale, species/muscle, population and specimen count, preparation and measurement temperature, loading/activation protocol, units, normalization, reference/current area and stress convention, uncertainty, source location, license and transformation hash. Label each input **measured**, **inverse fitted**, **reference-model parameter** or **authored assumption**, and map it to an explicit equation and code variable. Missing fields remain missing rather than inferred from a related muscle or preparation.

The present $\sigma_0$ multiplies the derivative of a full-stretch active potential; its directional Cauchy contribution also depends on activation, force–length factor, fibre stretch and $J$. A comparison with single-fibre force/area therefore requires matching configurations and area conventions. The three 3.60/8.71/20.38 MPa coefficients match recorded model-force targets in unresolved fixtures; they are not measured physiological specific tensions. PCSA, geometric $V/L$, effective numerical $F/\sigma_0$ and microscopic fibre area must remain distinct.

The staged research revision should connect fibre architecture and packing to aggregate muscle parameters, then separate force–length, force–velocity and load/hold/release evidence. Activation kinetics need their own calibration. Elastic storage and release need to be separated from measured hysteretic absorption or creep/relaxation. First resolve the isolated displacement/pressure and force-stationarity prerequisites documented above; broader constitutive or rate changes require a source-bound research plan and their own material tests, calibration and trajectories. No such broader model change is implemented here.


## Declared aggregation route for the next property lesson

A continuum need not resolve each fibre. For a **reference material bundle** of volume $V_0$ (m³), representative fibre length $L_{f,0}$ (m), and a declared contractile packing fraction $\phi_0$, the parallel-fibre idealization gives $A_{\mathrm{contractile},0}=\phi_0 V_0/L_{f,0}$ (m²). Under homogeneous identical fibre cross-sections $A_{f,0}$, an idealized count is $n=A_{\mathrm{contractile},0}/A_{f,0}$; equivalently a count per reference cross-sectional area times that area. This is an assumed aggregation model, not a measurement of fibre number. For physiological cross-section, preserve the source's fibre-length and pennation conventions: when $\sigma_f$ is fibre tension per contractile area, tendon-directed force in a uniform idealization is $F_t=\sigma_f A_{\mathrm{contractile}}\cos\alpha$. If a measured whole-muscle effective tension already includes noncontractile fractions, multiplying by packing fraction again double counts that reduction. Distributions of orientation, fibre type, force–length response and load sharing require a declared averaging rule.

A tracked material bundle can shorten, thicken and rotate during acute contraction. A fixed anatomical section can be crossed by different material and fibres as they move. Neither the changing section nor apparent bulging alone warrants a force-capacity bonus. Reference/current area conventions must be reconciled with the stress measure before mapping fibre measurements into a whole-muscle constitutive coefficient. Mass density in kg/m³ independently maps reference volume to mass; neither diameter nor force/area measurements supply it.

[Raiteri et al. (2016), original three-dimensional ultrasound study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4974924/) supplies an architectural example from human **tibialis anterior**, not the atlas biceps. Twelve participants performed isometric contractions at rest and 5–50% maximal voluntary contraction with the knee extended and ankle at 90°. Tracking and segmentation showed regional bulging, fascicle shortening and increased pennation with near-constant measured volume; usable sample sizes varied by quantity. That motivates longitudinally varying geometry and distinct area conventions, not parameter transfer between muscles.

Endomysium, perimysium and other extracellular matrix supply shear and lateral load paths absent from the simple axial bar. Fibre packing is not myosin filament spacing. Intracellular/interstitial fluid is distinct from blood in vessels; perfusion depends on more than the current body's finite bulk penalty. Fluid transport, vascular/perfusion effects, extracellular matrix calibration, exercise pump, edema and chronic hypertrophy are **deferred**. No one of those processes is silently represented by the acute isochoric toggle or active-stress offset.
