## Separate measurement from mechanics

| Evidence quantity | Permissible future constraint | Missing information / prohibited shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MHC-protein fraction | Composition prior for the source population, retaining named muscle/head identity | Does not equal fiber count or contractile area. Cannot multiply a whole-muscle force target by this percentage to obtain measured type-specific force. |
| Fiber-count fraction and type-specific mean fiber area | Under representative cross-sectional sampling, area weights may be computed as `w_i = c_i A_i / sum(c_j A_j)` | Requires comparable sampling and hybrid/other fibers. Protein fractions cannot substitute for `c_i`; a reported smallest diameter is not an assumed circular area. |

| Single-fiber specific tension and force–velocity measurements | Constrain a stress or rate law after matching area/stress convention, activation, stretch, shortening normalization, preparation and temperature | Do not transfer a skinned-fiber curve or older-cohort mean as an atlas-subject default. | Arm26 maximum-isometric-force parameter | Retains a separately identified model-reference target | A matched actuator parameter is not measured force, measured PCSA or a validated continuum calibration of BodyParts3D. |

## Relate a future measurement to the current coefficient

The unchanged implemented active term has `P_active = a sigma0 f_L(lambda) n tensor f0`, where `lambda = |F f0|` and `n = F f0 / lambda`. Its active Cauchy stress is therefore `sigma_active = a sigma0 f_L(lambda) lambda/J (n tensor n)`. This restates the current derivative in [anatomical-material.mjs](../../web/anatomical-material.mjs); it is not a new constitutive assumption. Comparing `sigma0` directly to force/current-area measurements without `a`, stretch, the force–length factor, `J` and orientation would compare different quantities. Passive matrix/fiber/volume stresses and attachment/contact reactions also affect the measured boundary resultant.

Under a separately declared parallel, common-strain population model, a possible aggregation is `F_t = A_contractile sum(w_i sigma_i(a_i, lambda_i, v_i) cos(alpha_i))`. If a source defines PCSA with a pennation projection already included, the cosine must not be applied twice. Reference/current area, packing and stress conventions must be chosen consistently. This equation is a proposed interpretation framework, not implemented mechanics. It neither supplies an area mixture from protein fractions nor grants an extra capacity factor from acute bulging.

The existing source-bound calibration diagnostic records effective `F/sigma0` divided by geometric `V/L` ratios **0.570477** for brachialis, **0.112488** for short biceps and **0.0550363** for long biceps. These are properties of unresolved fitted fixtures, not inferred contractile packing fractions or measured PCSA. Their fitted stress coefficients **3.59933, 8.70839 and 20.37812 MPa** remain model-derived quantities. The large differences make consistent architecture/area conventions and sufficiently rich displacement fields prerequisites for interpreting or replacing them.

## A source-bound calibration sequence

1. Record one row per proposed input: muscle/head, structural scale, cohort and sample count, protocol/preparation/temperature, measured quantity, units, area and stress convention, uncertainty, source location and transformation hash. Missing values remain missing. Label measured inputs, reference-model targets, inverse fits and authored assumptions separately.
2. Resolve isolated fixture displacement/pressure and integration sensitivity before fitting a new coefficient. Require fresh force stationarity and geometry evidence in the enriched field; the old force match alone does not identify material tension, PCSA or bulk behavior.
3. Select an explicit architecture/area mixture convention and quantify what its measurements can identify. Protein composition may constrain a population prior; force, PCSA, pennation, force–length, velocity and activation evidence need their own constraints. Do not infer all of them from one fixed-end target.
4. Fit and test active capacity, passive response, tendon/aponeurosis response and volumetric behavior against their distinct protocols. Fiber composition does not identify the bulk modulus or repair contact curvature. Existing finite-bulk results remain diagnostic controls.
5. If a law changes, revise its equations, book and applicable narrow Lean statements, run independent derivative/material tests, re-solve fixed-end calibration and loaded trajectories, then report matched-time, spatial and quadrature sensitivity. Retain every old failed fixture and numerical bound.
